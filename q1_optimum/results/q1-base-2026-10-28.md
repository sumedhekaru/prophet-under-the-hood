# Q1 · Does Prophet's default fit reach the optimum of its own model? (2026-10-28)

**Answer:** not with 100 or more rows. There Prophet fits with L-BFGS, which stops short of the optimum (MAP) on every
dataset tested. Running it to convergence does not close the gap; Newton nearly does. Stopping short moves the
forecast by 1% or more on most series, but average holdout accuracy is the same within 2 SE on 10 of 11 datasets.
Below 100 rows Prophet uses Newton, and the default fit is at the optimum.

## Setup

200 seeded series per dataset (docs/evidence-plan.md), Prophet's default model (`base`), four fits per series:

- **S0** `Prophet().fit(df)`: what users run (Newton below 100 rows, L-BFGS from 100).
- **S1** L-BFGS run to convergence: `iter=100000`, absolute tolerances 1e-12 with the relative ones off. When
  cmdstan's line search fails at that tolerance, 1e-10 and then 1e-8 are tried.
- **S2** Newton: `fit(df, algorithm='Newton')`.
- **X** the exact MAP from prophet-batch 0.1.3 (`fit_many`).

lp is Prophet's log posterior (Stan's `lp__`: constants dropped), evaluated for all four fits by one function
(`common/model.py`), which matches Stan's `lp__` to the printed digits. The forecast change is the mean |forecast(X)
− forecast(S0)| over the holdout, as a share of the series' mean |y|. MASE is on the holdout (last 52 weeks, 365 days
or 24 months; M4: its own test set), with the seasonal-naive scale.

Commands: `sh q1_optimum/run.sh`, then `python -m q1_optimum.analyze ...` (the table below).

## Findings

1. **With L-BFGS, the default fit is never at the optimum.** Its lp is below X's on every L-BFGS dataset (median gap
   0.09–3.2, up to 61). X is never below either stock reference (column "X < max(S1, S2)": 0% everywhere), so X is
   the optimum the stock fits are aiming for.
2. **Running L-BFGS longer does not get there.** S1 gains a median of 2e-5 to 0.12 lp over S0. Newton (S2) gets most
   of the way (81–97% of X's median gain), at 11–37× S0's time on daily data. The likely
   reason is the Laplace prior on the changepoints (|δ| is not differentiable at 0), which L-BFGS handles poorly. Not
   tested further here.
3. **The gap moves the forecast.** On L-BFGS datasets the forecast at the optimum differs from the default fit's by
   1% or more on 63–85% of series (median 1.5–5.6%, p90 up to 38%). M4 Daily is the exception (6%: long, smooth
   series).
4. **It does not move accuracy on average.** X − S0 MASE is within 2 SE of 0 on 10 of 11 datasets. On Wikipedia
   weekly the optimum is 0.9% worse (+0.0100 ± 0.0078). So the forecasts differ series by series, but neither fit
   is better on the whole.
5. **Below 100 rows the default fit is at the optimum** (Newton: median gap 0.001–0.003 lp). Even so, 20–26% of
   forecasts differ by 1% or more for gaps that small: these short models have flat optima, where a small change in
   lp moves the forecast.

## Limits

- Prophet's default model only (no holidays or regressors); Q2 adds holidays and multiplicative seasonality.
- 200 series per dataset; the paired differences carry their standard errors.
- One machine (Apple M4); optimizer paths may differ slightly on other platforms.

Environment: prophet 1.5.0, prophet-batch 0.1.3, numpy 2.5.4, pandas 3.1.0, Python 3.12.13, macOS-26.5.2-arm64-arm-64bit, repo e404142 plus the then-uncommitted code, committed unchanged as 982ac16
